Chapter OneThe Laws of Demand & Supply
Buyers outnumber the grain → price climbs. Grain is plentiful → price falls.

Welcome, Sarpanchji. Grain has no fixed price in Tawapur — it is set by demand and supply. When buyers outnumber the grain on the market the price climbs; when grain is plentiful it falls. Read that balance, and your treasury — funded by tax alone — will grow.

  • 18% indirect tax on every good sold at the Shop.
  • Income tax on educated workers.
  • The Farm harvests grain. Villagers eat from a personal store and restock at the Shop.
  • Every 5 days a census runs: with enough goods and housing, two babies are born.
  • The School needs 2–5 Basic teachers to lift uneducated workers to Basic. Reach 75% Basic Education to unlock the College.

One day passes each real minute. Press space to pause or resume, and click buildings to inspect them.

Chapter TwoGovernment Intervention
Release grain → price falls (treasury earns). Procure grain → price rises (treasury pays).

A new lever is yours, Sarpanchji: the Panchayat Godown, a public grain reserve. With it you can lean against the very market Chapter One set in motion.

  • Release grain (flow up): you sell from the reserve onto the market. Supply rises, the price falls, and the sale earns your treasury ₹.
  • Procure grain (flow down): you buy from the market into the reserve. Supply tightens, the price rises, and it costs the treasury ₹.
  • You cannot release from an empty reserve, nor procure with an empty treasury.
  • Use the − / + on the Godown card to set the daily flow; hold at zero to let the free market be.

The Panchayat Godown now sits in your Build & Assign panel.
