Book One · The Basics
Chapter OneThe Laws of Demand & Supply
Buyers outnumber the grain → price climbs. Grain is plentiful → price falls.

Welcome, Sarpanchji. Grain has no fixed price in Tawapur — it is set by demand and supply. When buyers outnumber the grain on the market the price climbs; when grain is plentiful it falls. Read that balance, and your treasury — funded by tax alone — will grow.

  • 18% indirect tax on every good sold at the Shop.
  • Income tax on educated workers.
  • The Farm harvests grain. Villagers eat from a personal store and restock at the Shop.
  • Every 5 days a census runs: with enough grain and housing, two babies are born.
  • The School needs 2–5 Basic teachers to lift uneducated workers to Basic. Reach 75% Basic Education to unlock the College wing.

One day passes each real minute. Press space to pause or resume, and click buildings to inspect them.

Chapter TwoGovernance
Release grain → price falls (treasury earns). Procure grain → price rises (treasury pays).

A new lever is yours, Sarpanchji: the Panchayat Godown, a public grain reserve. With it you can lean against the very market Chapter One set in motion.

  • Release grain (flow up): you sell from the reserve onto the market. Supply rises, the price falls, and the sale earns your treasury ₹.
  • Procure grain (flow down): you buy from the market into the reserve. Supply tightens, the price rises, and it costs the treasury ₹.
  • You cannot release from an empty reserve, nor procure with an empty treasury.
  • Use the − / + on the Godown card to set the daily flow; hold at zero to let the free market be.

The Panchayat Godown now sits in your Production panel.

Chapter ThreeInstitutions and Services
Good times → savings flow to your treasury. Withdrawals return with 3% interest.

With 75% of Tawapur now educated, the College wing can be added — and new institutions open to you, Sarpanchji. Chief among them: a village Bank.

  • The Bank (₹2,000) lets citizens deposit excess cash in good times. Those deposits flow straight into your treasury to spend.
  • Deposits are locked for one year. When hard times come, citizens withdraw — and you must repay from the treasury with 3% interest.
  • A Billing Counter (₹100) adds 2 more shopkeepers to the Shop, easing queues and lifting tax throughput.
  • Both the Bank and the Billing Counter are managed from the Service panel.

Beware over-leveraging: if you spend deposits you cannot repay, a run on the vault can drain your treasury when withdrawals come due.

Book Two · Beyond the Village
Chapter FourSeasons of Change
The bus links Tawapur to the region — residents ride out, visitors come in to spend.

Tawapur has thrived — 30 souls at high spirits for a full year. Now life turns with the seasons, and the village gains new layers: a Dispensary to treat the sick, and a Bus Stop for journeys beyond the fields.

  • The year now turns through five seasons of six days each — Winter, Spring, Summer, Monsoon (the rains arrive) and Autumn. The current season shows beside the date.
  • Spring is planting and Autumn is the harvest — the whole year's grain is gathered then, set by your farmers and implements, and it must last until the next harvest. Ration it.
  • Through the offseason (Winter, Summer, Monsoon) the fields rest and your farmhands are laid off — redeploy them to other work, or let them wait for Spring.
  • On holidays, villagers ride off-map for a small fare (to your treasury) and come back refreshed — a morale & productivity lift for three days. While away, they leave the workforce.
  • Visitors arrive to spend surplus grain at the shop, dhaba & bar, paying both direct and indirect tax — outside money flowing into Tawapur.
  • Every villager now carries a health meter. The sick walk to the Dispensary if a dose is in stock and they can afford it (₹10–50 of their own); if not, their output and morale slide until they recover.
  • Build the Dispensary in the village and keep it stocked with medicine (₹5/g) from your treasury, so no one is turned away.
  • Any villager whose morale falls to 20% now downs tools and refuses to work.
  • Prosperity makes room to grow: one extra house plot opened with Chapter Three, and two more open now.

The Bus Stop awaits in your Service panel, Sarpanchji.
